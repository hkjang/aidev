Take a heading's closing hashes off its name

Markdown lets a heading be written closed — "## 분기 요약 ##" — and atxHeading
counted only the hashes at the front, leaving the ones at the end as letters of
the title. The first heading of a document becomes the deck's name, so a title
written that way filed the deck under "분기 요약 ##" and put that locator after
the file on every slide's citation; the author's only remedy was to delete
characters markdown says are not there.

A space in front of the run is what makes it punctuation, as CommonMark has it,
and insisting on the space is what keeps "# C# 도입" and "# 제목#" whole — a
heading naming a language after a hash, and a word spelled with one. Nothing
but hashes after the opening run ("## ###") is markdown's empty heading, which
this reader already had a shape for.

Out of scope: a closing sequence escaped with a backslash ("# 제목 \###"), which
CommonMark reads as "제목 ###". The run here ends on the backslash rather than a
space, so that line keeps reading exactly as it did.

The leading-hash count (1-6) and the "space or end of line after the hashes"
rule are untouched, as are the deck DSL's own "#" headings in
internal/deck/source.go, deckWriter, and the number parsers.
